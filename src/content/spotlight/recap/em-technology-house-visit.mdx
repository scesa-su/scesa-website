On Friday 12 June 2026, students from the Strathmore School of Computing and Engineering Sciences sat down with the engineers at E&M Technology House Ltd to see how software for banks and fintech companies is built and run at scale. We organised the visit together with the E&M team.

## Banking software, explained by the people who build it

The engineers started with core banking platforms, the systems a bank runs on to keep accounts and move money. From there they moved to Banking as a Service (BaaS) and embedded finance.

If those terms are new to you, here is the short version. BaaS is when a licensed bank lets other companies use its banking features through software, so those companies can offer accounts or payments without becoming a bank themselves. Embedded finance is what you see on the other side: payments, loans or other financial services built straight into an app or platform that isn't a bank.

## The hard parts

A lot of the session went into the problems that come with handling other people's money. The team talked through cybersecurity, vulnerability assessment and penetration testing (VAPT), open banking, and regulatory compliance. They framed each one as a real-world challenge their teams deal with.

The session ended with a live walkthrough of applications running in production, so we got to see the systems behind everything we had just heard about.

## Why we keep doing these

SCESA exists partly to close the gap between what we learn in class and what employers expect on the job. This visit was one step toward that, and we want to keep working with companies in the industry so our members are ready to contribute from their first day at work.
